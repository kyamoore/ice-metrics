Analytics & Modeling Methodology

How ICE//METRICS evaluates player performance, shot danger, and expected goal probabilities

1. Expected Goals (xG) Shot Evaluation Model

The Expected Goals (xG) model assigns a probability value (ranging from 0.00 to 1.00) to every unblocked shot attempt based on historical likelihood of becoming a goal. Rather than treating all shots equally, our model isolates spatial coordinate data and game context factors:

  • Shot Distance & Angle: Euclidean distance to the center of the net and the acute angle off the goal line.
  • Shot Type & Event Sequence: Distinguishes high-probability shot types (slap shots, wrist shots, tip-ins, deflection, snap shots, backhands) and pre-shot speed/rebound tracking.
  • Game State Context: Adjusts probabilities based on strength state (5v5, 5v4 power play, 4v5 shorthanded) and score differential.
On-Ice xG Share Formula: xGF% = [ xGF / (xGF + xGA) ] × 100

Where xGF = Expected Goals For while on ice, and xGA = Expected Goals Allowed.

2. Shot Danger Zone Classification (HDCF%)

To evaluate territorial quality, shot attempts are categorized into three spatial danger tiers based on slot proximity and rebound likelihood:

High Danger (HD)

Inner slot and crease area (shots within ~10–15 ft and crease rebounds). Historical Conversion: >15%

Medium Danger (MD)

Outer slot and high-slot perimeter up to the faceoff dots. Historical Conversion: ~6–12%

Low Danger (LD)

Point shots near the blueline and acute perimeter angles. Historical Conversion: <3%

3. PDO Regression & Contextual Score Effects

Raw statistics can be distorted by luck or tactical score effects (e.g., teams trailing late take more aggressive shot risks). ICE//METRICS applies two fundamental adjustments:

PDO / Luck Normalization

Calculated as $\text{On-Ice Shooting \%} + \text{On-Ice Save \%}$. Over 82 games, team PDO regresses toward 100.0. A PDO above 102.5 signals unsustainable shooting luck, while a PDO below 97.5 indicates negative variance.

Score & Venue Adjustments

Shot attempt data is weighted based on score differential (e.g., shot attempts while trailing are downweighted slightly to account for artificial possession volume generated during trailing push states).

Season & Playoff Architecture

How a standard NHL season and Stanley Cup Playoff tournament operate

The 82-Game Regular Season

Runs from October through mid-April. Teams earn 2 points for a win (regulation, overtime, or shootout), 1 point for an overtime/shootout loss (OTL), and 0 points for a regulation loss.

The 16-Team Stanley Cup Playoffs

The top 3 teams in each division (12 teams) plus 2 Wild Card teams per conference (4 teams) advance to the postseason. Four rounds are played as best-of-7 series to decide the Stanley Cup champion.

Statistical Definitions Glossary

Explanations of traditional and advanced metrics used across ICE//METRICS

GP (Games Played) & G / A / PTS

G (Goals): Total pucks shot directly into the opponent's net.
A (Assists): Passes directly leading to a teammate's goal (up to 2 per goal).
PTS (Points): Total Goals + Assists combined.

P/GP (Points Per Game)

Calculated as $\text{PTS} / \text{GP}$. Normalizes scoring output to evaluate player scoring pace independently of games missed due to injury or rest.

Sh% (Shooting Percentage)

The percentage of on-target shots on goal that result in goals ($\text{Goals} / \text{Shots on Goal} \times 100$). League average is roughly 9.5%–10.5%.

Corsi For (CF%) & Fenwick For (FF%)

Corsi (CF%): On-ice total shot attempt differential (Shots + Misses + Blocked Shots).
Fenwick (FF%): On-ice unblocked shot attempt differential (Shots + Misses).
